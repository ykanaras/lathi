How to Make Handmade Soap Last Longer

By Lathi Soap · September 27, 2026

The most important way to make handmade soap last longer is simple: let the bar drain and dry between uses. A draining soap dish, protection from the direct shower stream, and dry storage for unused bars can help you enjoy every bar more fully.

Handmade soap is meant to meet water, create lather, and be used. But when a wet bar sits in pooled water or stays under the shower spray, it continues to soften even after you have finished washing. A few easy habits can reduce that unnecessary wear.

These tips work whether you keep one favorite by the sink or choose several fragrances through our mix-and-match offer of five soaps for $30.

1. Use a soap dish that drains

Choose a dish with raised ridges, slots, or drainage holes so water can move away from the bar. A flat dish without drainage can leave the underside sitting in water, which makes the surface softer and messier.

The exact material matters less than the design. Wood, ceramic, metal, and silicone dishes can all work when they keep the bar elevated and allow water to escape.

2. Keep the bar out of the direct shower stream

A shower shelf may look convenient, but constant splashing can keep the soap wet long after you use it. Place the dish where the bar is easy to reach but not directly beneath the showerhead.

If your shower has limited space, move the bar to a dry ledge or outside the shower after use. That small step can make a noticeable difference in how quickly the surface dries.

3. Give the wet bar airflow

After washing, place the bar on its broad side or on a raised surface with room for air to move around it. Avoid closing a damp bar inside a container immediately. A covered soap box is helpful for travel, but the bar should dry before the lid stays closed.

In a shared bathroom, leaving a little space between different bars also prevents them from sticking together and makes it easier to identify each scent.

4. Apply soap to a washcloth or bath accessory

Instead of holding the bar under running water for the entire wash, briefly wet it and rub it onto a damp washcloth or your preferred bath accessory. Then set the bar back on its draining dish while you continue washing.

This keeps the soap from spending extra time in the water and lets you control how much you use. Rinse and dry the washcloth or accessory according to its care instructions.

5. Keep the soap dish clean

Soap residue can block drainage slots and create a damp layer beneath the bar. Rinse the dish regularly, remove buildup, and let the dish dry before returning the soap when practical.

If the bar has become soft, do not throw it away. Move it to a clean, dry dish with good airflow and allow it time to firm up before using it again.

6. Store unopened bars somewhere cool and dry

Extra bars do not belong on a wet shower shelf. Keep them in a dry cabinet, drawer, or basket away from splashing water, high humidity, and direct sunlight. Avoid airtight storage if the bars are not completely dry.

When you buy several scents, leave each bar identifiable so you know what you have. Our current collection includes year-round favorites and limited-time seasonal choices, so a simple label can help you save a particular fragrance for later.

7. Let travel soap dry before packing

When traveling, shake off excess water and give the bar time to dry before closing it in a travel case. A case with drainage or ventilation is useful during the trip, but it should still be kept out of pooled water.

For the trip home, wrap only a dry bar. If it is still damp at checkout time, keep the travel case slightly open until the bar has had a chance to dry, then secure it before placing it in your luggage.

Quick answers about handmade soap care

Should handmade soap be kept in the shower?

It can be, as long as it is on a draining dish and outside the direct stream of water. A drier location is better when the shower stays humid for long periods.

Can I store several soap bars together?

Yes, if the bars are dry and have some airflow. Keep wet bars separated so they do not stick together, and preserve labels when you want to remember each scent.

What should I do with small leftover pieces?

Press a softened small piece onto a damp new bar and let them dry together, or collect the pieces in a soap-saver pouch designed for washing. Either approach helps you use the bar down to the end.
